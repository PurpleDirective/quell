Quell is on.

It hides the AI answers you didn’t ask for and leaves everything else alone.

Your browser hasn’t let Quell work on Google and Bing yet.

  • Search On

    Hides the AI Overview above Google results, AI Mode, Gemini and Bing Copilot. Answers you open yourself in “People also ask” stay. DuckDuckGo, Brave and Yahoo are one switch away.

  • Cookie banners Off

    Hides “we use cookies” notices, and says “reject all” for you where the site uses a consent tool Quell knows.

  • Pop-ups Off

    Newsletter sign-up boxes, chat bubbles and “open in app” banners.

Turn on cookie banners and pop-ups?

They need access to all sites. Your browser asks once. Quell never records the pages you visit, and gives the access back if you turn both off.

A random install ID, Quell version and browser, which switches are on, how many items each feature hid, popup opens and button clicks, and error messages. Never the pages you visit, your searches or your browsing history.

Pin Quell to your toolbar

example.com

Open the puzzle-piece menu, then pin the crescent. Every switch works straight away. If a site looks wrong, pause Quell for that site from the same place.

  • Hide a real search result. Every release is tested against that.
  • Record the pages you visit. Quell works on your device. Anonymous usage counts are sent only if you allow them, and never include pages, searches or history.
  • Sell or share data, or use it for anything but fixing and improving Quell.
  • Hold on to access it isn’t using. Turn Cookie banners and Pop-ups both off and Quell gives its all-sites access back.

Your settings sync through your own browser account, like bookmarks do. Apart from usage counts you allow, the one thing Quell sends is a “this site looks broken” report you choose to send: just the site’s domain and Quell’s version, shown to you before it goes.
