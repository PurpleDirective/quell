Quell is on.

It hides the AI answers, cookie banners and pop-ups you didn’t ask for — and leaves everything else alone.

Your browser hasn’t let Quell work on Google and Bing yet.

Three switches, your call

  • Search On

    Hides the AI Overview above Google results, AI Mode, Gemini and Bing Copilot. An answer you open yourself in “People also ask” stays — you asked for it. DuckDuckGo, Brave and Yahoo are one switch away.

  • Cookie banners Off until you turn it on

    Hides “we use cookies” pop-ups, and can say “reject all” for you where the site uses a consent tool Quell knows.

  • Pop-ups Off until you turn it on

    Newsletter sign-up boxes, chat bubbles and “open in app” banners.

What Quell will never do

  • Hide a real search result. Every release is tested against that.
  • Collect your browsing. Quell works on your device and keeps nothing about the pages you visit.
  • Sell or share data, or use it for anything but fixing Quell.
  • Hold on to access it isn’t using. Turn Cookie banners and Pop-ups both off and Quell gives its all-sites access back.

Your settings sync through your own browser account, like bookmarks do. The one thing Quell ever sends to us is a “this site looks broken” report you choose to send — just the site’s domain and Quell’s version, shown to you before it goes.

Changing things

Click Quell’s crescent icon in your toolbar. If you don’t see it, open your browser’s extensions menu (the puzzle piece) and pin Quell. Every switch works straight away — no reload needed — and if a site ever looks wrong, pause Quell for just that site from the same place.
